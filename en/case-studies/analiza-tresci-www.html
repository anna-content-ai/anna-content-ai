Website copy review: from promises to specifics

A review of the copy on a landing page for an AI website builder. I looked for unsupported promises and unclear buttons, then compared how two AI models, ChatGPT and Claude, handle the same task.

In short

AI is good at spotting risky phrases, but when rewriting it can add new, unconfirmed details. A human chooses the final version.

“Most revolutionary”, “millions of users”, “guaranteed results”: each of these needs proof or has to go.

My editorial assessment

  1. Claude gave the stronger diagnosis: it explained in detail the risks to trust and the pressure language.
  2. But when rewriting it added unconfirmed details, e.g. “Answer a few simple questions” and “No coding or design experience required”.
  3. ChatGPT rewrote the copy more carefully and stuck to the given user journey (“Create your account”).
  4. But its checklist was overconfident: it marked as done areas that couldn’t be checked without brand guidelines and testing.

The best result comes from combining them: Claude’s risk analysis, ChatGPT’s caution when rewriting, product verification by a human, and final editorial approval.
