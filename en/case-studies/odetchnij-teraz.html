Odetchnij Teraz: an app for everyday stress

A concept for a mobile app with short breathing and grounding exercises and small habits. Odetchnij Teraz means “Breathe Now”. The idea: one clear action per screen and language that supports rather than promises a cure.

My role

I turned the team’s shared concept into interactive screens and flows in Figma Make, wrote the UX copy, and ran reviews against Nielsen’s heuristics and for accessibility.

The challenge

Many wellbeing apps have big content libraries, long onboarding and long sessions. When someone feels overwhelmed, that’s a barrier. The project had to:

  • reduce cognitive effort and show the main action right away,
  • support quick stress-regulation exercises,
  • encourage habits without pressure,
  • speak calmly and supportively, without medical language or diagnoses.

Product principles

  • 01Less loadOne clear next step, short instructions, a limited number of choices.
  • 02Small stepsA short breathing session or one completed habit already counts as progress.
  • 03Supportive languageNo guilt, pressure or exaggerated promises. What you can do now.
  • 04User choiceOwn exercises, times of day, reminders and habits.
  • 05No medical overreachThe app doesn’t diagnose, treat or replace therapy.

Breathing sessionShort visual and text cues. An SOS exercise without complicated counters.
CompletionGentle feedback, no competition or points.
HabitFrom a ready exercise or from scratch: name, frequency, reminder, minimum goal, icon.
ProgressBrief and in the background. Journal and history in a separate section.

How I worked with AI

The screens were built in Figma Make with vibe coding: I used iterative prompts to create, test and refine the interfaces. AI was a design assistant, not the decision-maker. I reviewed every generated version by hand.

My contribution

  • Screens and flows: the team’s concept turned into an interactive prototype (onboarding, home screen, breathing session, habits, journal, progress).
  • Information architecture: navigation hierarchy and the order of steps.
  • UX copy: microcopy plus consistent labels, buttons and messages.
  • Reviews: Nielsen’s heuristics, simplifying key tasks, testing the main path.
  • Accessibility: contrast, semantic colours, touch targets and all element states.

What I learned

Fast AI generation works very well for creating and comparing screen variants. The best results, though, came only from combining clear constraints, a review based on usability principles, and deliberate priorities set by a human.
